## The post (word count: 229, hashtags not counted)

Most nights, around 11pm, I call home.

Papa picks up on the fourth ring. I ask how the day was. He says, "Sab theek hai, beta." Mummy says the same. And the call ends.

I hear the words. I do not hear the week.

The check-up that got moved. The bill that is due. The strange call asking for an OTP. The papers that are somewhere in a drawer. My sister remembers half of it. I remember the other half. Nobody has the whole picture, least of all the two people it is about.

So I built Parwah (परवाह).

It is a free family app, made in India. One place for the family: a daily "I am okay" tap, shared to-dos, doctors, medicines and papers, an emergency card, and an SOS that reaches everyone at once.

Papa gets three big buttons and nothing else to learn. And nothing happens without his yes: he switches on what he wants to share, and nobody joins our family unless one of us lets them in.

It is not about keeping watch on our parents. It is about being there, together, even from another city.

It is live today, and free for families. If you try it this Diwali, I would love to hear what works and what does not.

What is the one thing that would make caring for your parents easier?

## Reply bank (for the first two hours)

- **"Is it really free?"** Yes. Free for families today. A paid Family Plan with extras is coming later; what you use today stays free.
- **"Android or iPhone?"** Both. It runs in the phone's browser, and Add to Home Screen gives it its own icon. It is not in the app stores yet.
- **"My parents are not good with phones."** That is why their screen has three big buttons. Set it up together the next time you are home.
- **"Is this tracking?"** No. Location sharing is off until the parent turns it on themselves, and stopping deletes it.
- **"What about our data?"** ID papers keep only the last 4 digits. Nothing is sold or shared with advertisers. The privacy page says exactly what is kept.
- **"Does it call an ambulance?"** No. SOS alerts the family and gives one tap to 112. It is not an emergency service.
