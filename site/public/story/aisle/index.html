The day you notice.

Story · The aisle

Nobody tells you when your parents get older. You just notice, one ordinary day, and you cannot un-notice it.

Maybe it is on a video call, when Papa takes a second longer to get up from the sofa and waves it off before you can say anything. Maybe it is during a visit home, when you see Mummy keeping one hand on the wall on her way to the bathroom at night. Nothing dramatic has happened. Nothing needs an ambulance. But something has shifted, and you felt it.

If you live in another city, or another country, that feeling has a particular weight. You are the one they called when the inverter stopped working and when the bank app asked for an OTP. But for this, they will never call. Asking for help with the everyday feels, to them, like giving something up.

The hardest part of caring for parents is not the big decisions. It is the hundred small ones nobody talks about.

What we found when we went looking

So you do what our generation does: you open a shopping app at midnight and search. And what you find is grim. Grey hospital plastic. Products photographed like equipment, described like machinery, sold like an apology. Listings with no weight ratings, no install guidance, no answer to the only question that matters: will this actually be safe for my mother?

Everything about that aisle says the same quiet thing: this stage of life is a problem to be managed. We do not accept that. Sixty is not an ending. Our parents are still hosting, still arguing about politics, still spoiling grandchildren, still living. What they need is not management. It is a little thoughtful backup, chosen well, explained clearly, delivered with respect.

So we are building the aisle

परवाह Parwah is the Hindi word for care. Not the hospital kind. The everyday kind: the sweater your mother made you carry, the extra roti on your plate, the "message me when you land". They spent decades on that kind of care. Parwah exists so you can send some of it back.

We are starting with a short, honest range of daily-living essentials, ortho comfort, bathroom safety, and small aids that keep routines independent, and we hold every product to rules we wrote before we picked a single one.

Would we give it to our own parents?

If the answer is not an immediate yes, it does not make the catalogue. That is the whole rule, and it disqualifies more than you would think.

Safety you can verify

Every grab bar, stool and seat ships with its tested load rating and plain-language install guidance. No certificate on file, no listing. You should not have to take our word for it, and with Parwah you do not have to.

No fear, no pity

We will never sell you a product by making your parents look helpless. And we make no medical claims of any kind: comfort, stability and independence are promises we can keep, and we will not make ones we cannot.

Help that speaks their language

WhatsApp support in Hindi and English, for you or for them, whichever of you finds it easier. A real person, in daytime IST, who answers the question that was actually asked.
