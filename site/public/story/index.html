The 11pm phone call every one of us knows.

Why we're building Parwah · 4 min read

"Sab theek hai." Everything is fine. That's how the call always ends - and you hang up in your flat in Gurgaon, or Bengaluru, or Toronto, not quite believing it. Because last month "everything is fine" turned out to include a slip near the bathroom door that nobody mentioned until the bruise had already faded.

We are a generation that moved for work and stayed for life. Our parents stayed where our childhoods are. And between those two facts sits a quiet, daily worry that nobody has built for: the ordinary physics of an ageing home. A wet bathroom floor. A staircase with no railing on one side. A jar of achaar that won't open, so it simply gets put back.

Here's what we noticed: Indian homes already retrofit love. A folded towel becomes a bathmat. A plastic stool travels into the shower. A walking stick is bought from the chemist in a hurry, chosen in thirty seconds, wrong height, never adjusted. The care is enormous. The tools are jugaad.

The care is enormous. The tools are jugaad.

Elsewhere, this problem has an aisle

In the US and Japan, "ageing at home" is a whole category - grab bars designed like bathroom fittings rather than hospital hardware, kitchen tools built for stiffer hands, clothing with magnetic buttons that still looks like clothing. Not medical equipment. Daily-living equipment. Products that assume growing older is normal, because it is.

In India, that aisle mostly doesn't exist. What exists is scattered: a surgical shop that feels like a hospital corridor, a marketplace listing with no one to answer questions, an imported product with an English-only manual in six-point type. The person who cares (you) and the person who needs (them) are in different cities - and nothing in between is built for that shape of family.

So we're building the aisle

Parwah is a small, stubborn idea: choose the best daily-living products for Indian homes and Indian parents, test them for grip and weight and large-print honesty, and deliver them the way a family member would - with a card in your words, a how-to video in Hindi behind a QR code, and a real human on WhatsApp when questions come up.

We start with the three worries we heard most: the bathroom floor, the knees on the stairs, and the small daily struggles that chip away at independence. No exaggerated promises anywhere on the box - we sell safety, comfort and ease, and we let doctors do the doctoring.

The name says the rest. Parwah - परवाह - is the Hindi word for care. Our parents spent decades proving theirs. This is a way to return it, one well-chosen, well-explained, well-delivered object at a time.
